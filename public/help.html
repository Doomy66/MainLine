How to play Mainline

You rule a Main, a chain of worlds a jump-1 ship can cross, and every other Main in the sector is ruled by somebody else. Most of them are the computer. Build a fleet, take worlds and hold on to your own. One turn is a day and a jump takes a week.

Your first game

  1. New game. Driftwater Reach is a good first sector. Leave the area as the whole sector. The table lists every Main that is a faction, with its size, its capital's profile, its best tech level, what it earns a week and the credits it starts with. Click a Main, in the table or on the map, to take it. A big, rich Main is easier. One whose capital has a class A starport can build its own starships.
  2. Leave the rules as they are and press Start the game. Starting wealth multiplies every faction's opening treasury, up to five times, for a bigger war from the first day; it does not change what worlds pay each week. For a first game you might set News travels to Instantly: it is easier to learn the game when you can see everything your fleets can.
  3. Buy your first fleet. For this one purchase the whole catalogue is on offer, whatever your worlds can build. Click a class's name to see its design sheet. A good start is two or three ships of jump-2 or better with real guns, such as a Broadsword or Patrol Corvette, plus something cheap to guard home. Keep a few MCr back for fuel and missiles. Press Done: sail.
  4. Find a target. Click nearby systems. The system pane shows who holds each world, what it is worth, and how strong its defences are. An independent world with weak defences, one or two jumps away, is a good first conquest.
  5. Set standing orders. On the Fleets tab, pick your fleet and choose the preset Seek and destroy. That fleet will now attack any enemy it meets and besiege any world it does not hold.
  6. Jump. Press Jump… and click the target on the map. Solid green rings are in range now. Dashed rings need stops on the way, and the fleet will refuel at each. Then press Wait for news, and the days run until your fleet arrives.
  7. Go in. Your fleet arrives at the jump point. Press Orbit to send it to the main world and end the day. Its guns will start on the defences. When they are silenced the world submits after a few more days, so long as your fleet stays.
  8. Keep going. Your treasury fills at the end of each week. Spend it at a shipyard, repair at worlds you hold, and look at the Reports tab for battles, sightings and captures. Click the name of a place in a report to go to it on the map.

The screen

The map is the sector as PlanetHex draws it. The wheel zooms, dragging moves it, and clicking picks a system. Worlds are dots, sized by population and coloured by what their surface is like: blue for water, tan for a dusty atmosphere, grey for airless rock, and a hollow ring where nobody lives. Hexes are washed in the colour of the faction that holds them, and a capital has a ring round it. Zoom in to see starports, hex numbers, naval bases (★) and gas giants.

Your fleets are arrows beside their worlds. Other factions' fleets are diamonds; a dashed diamond is where a fleet was last reported, not where it is now. The system pane says when each one arrived and when it was last seen, and lists fleets that have gone under Gone, with the days they were there. A fleet in jump space is a dot on a dashed line between two systems. A red dashed ring is a siege, and a burst marks a battle today.

TabWhat it is for

FleetsYour fleets, their orders, standing orders and ships. Tick ships to split them off into a new fleet; merge fleets in the same place.
ShipyardLay down new ships at a yard you hold, or at an independent one. Import designs from the Ship Designer.

Save writes the whole game to one .game file. The game also keeps itself in the browser, so Carry on on the title screen picks up where you left off.

A day

Give orders, then press End day. With several players, each takes a turn giving orders for the same day and hands the screen on. When the last is done, everybody's orders happen at once. The computer's factions give theirs at the same moment.

A fleet can do one of these in a day:

  • Move anywhere in its system: the main world's orbit, a gas giant, the planetoid belt or the jump point.
  • Jump to another system, or start a route of several jumps.
  • Refuel, going to wherever in the system it can.
  • Repair at a class A to D starport you hold.

Orders last until they are done, so a fleet on a route of four jumps needs telling once. Wait for news lets the days run, up to thirty, until something happens you should see: a battle, an arrival, a capture, a sighting, a ship commissioned.

Jumping and fuel

A jump takes a week. It burns a tenth of each ship's hull in fuel for every parsec, so a 400-ton ship needs 40 tons to jump one parsec and 80 to jump two. A fleet can jump as far as its shortest-legged ship, and no further than the fuel in every ship's tanks. Ships arrive at the system's jump point.

Nobody sells fuel to an enemy; skimming a gas giant still works. Jumping on unrefined fuel risks a rough jump, which arrives a few days late. A ship with a fuel processor refines its own.

Order a jump beyond range and the fleet plans a route through systems where every ship in it can refuel, and stops to fill up at each.

A Main is every world a jump-1 ship can reach, so a jump-1 ship can never leave its own Main. To attack anyone you need jump-2 or better, and so does every ship in the fleet.

Jump tenders

Ships with no jump drive, such as system defence boats, fighters and pinnaces, can still go to war if something carries them. A class A or B starport that will deal with you has jump tenders for hire. Tick Hire jump tenders on the fleet before choosing its jump. The tenders are rated jump-3, so the fleet jumps up to three parsecs, or less if another ship in it has a shorter drive. They charge Traveller's freight rates for every ton they carry, per jump: Cr1,000 a ton for one parsec, Cr1,600 for two, Cr2,600 for three. The fee is paid as each jump starts. The tenders stay with the fleet for the whole route, refuelling themselves, and leave when it arrives. Cancel the route part way and they leave there.

Standing orders

Your fleets meet the enemy while you are elsewhere, so each fleet carries standing orders that say what to do about it.

OrderWhat it does
EngageAttack any enemy fleet it meets, only fleets it clearly outguns, or nobody unless attacked first.
Target firstWhat its guns pick out: warships, the largest, the smallest, the most damaged, or unarmed ships.
BesiegeAttack the defences of any world it is orbiting and does not hold, and stay until it submits. A fleet on its way somewhere, or stopping to refuel on a route, leaves the world alone; it lays siege only when it has no orders or has been sent to the world's orbit.
InterceptGo after enemy fleets it would engage that are elsewhere in the same system.
EvadeTry to break off whenever attacked.
Withdraw atTry to break off once the fleet is down to this share of its hull.

Combat

Battle happens wherever hostile fleets are in the same place and one of them means to fight. At a main world, its defences join in. A day of battle is three rounds, and every weapon fires once a round.

  • Hitting. Roll two dice and add the gunner (+1), the ship's Fire Control software and its sensors. Take off the target's Evade software, which can be no more than its Thrust. Eight or more hits, and anything over eight is the Effect. Bays and spinal mounts are clumsy against small ships: −2 against anything up to 2,000 tons and −4 up to 100.
  • Damage. The weapon's dice plus the Effect, less the target's armour, times the mounting's multiple: 1 for a turret, 3 for a barbette, 10, 20 or 100 for a small, medium or large bay, and 1,000 for a spinal mount. Armour piercing weapons ignore some armour; meson guns ignore all of it.
  • Missiles and torpedoes come in salvos from a magazine that runs dry. They hit more easily, but point defence batteries and laser turrets shoot some down, and each one that gets through hits on its own. Reload at a class A to C starport you hold.
  • Defences. Sandcasters take a die off laser hits. Meson screens blunt meson guns; nuclear dampers blunt radiation weapons.
  • Critical hits. A ship takes one for every tenth of its hull it loses, and one for any hit with an Effect of 6 or more. They knock out weapons, drives, the jump drive, sensors, the computer and crew, or hole the fuel tanks. A ship with a wrecked jump drive cannot jump until repaired.
  • Breaking off. A fleet that wants out rolls 8+ on two dice, plus its Thrust less that of the fastest enemy chasing it. A fleet that gets away moves elsewhere in the system.

Every battle report on the Reports tab opens to show each round, hit and critical.

Taking a world

Every peopled world has defences: ground batteries and the local system defence boats. They are stronger with more people and a higher tech level, and a naval base adds a squadron's worth. A world of a billion people at TL12 is a fortress; a mining camp has almost nothing. The system pane shows what any world has.

  1. Bring armed ships into orbit with Besiege on in their standing orders.
  2. Silence the defences. That may take several days of battle.
  3. Hold the orbit, with none of the owner's armed ships there, until the world submits. That takes two days plus one for every two digits of population. The siege resets if you leave or are driven off.

A captured world's defences start at a quarter strength. Defences rebuild a tenth a day once nobody is shooting at them. Lose your capital and you rule from your next biggest world.

Money and shipyards

At the end of every week each world pays its owner. It pays more for more people, higher tech and a better starport, and more again for a rich, industrial or agricultural world. A billion people at TL12 with a class A port pay about MCr35 a week. The same week, the upkeep of your ships comes out.

A yard can only have so many ships on the slips at once: three at a class A starport, two at class B and one at class C. Order more and they wait their turn, each starting when a slip comes free; the Shipyard tab shows how busy a yard is and when each ship will start and finish. An independent yard's slips are shared by everybody who builds there.

Repairs happen at worlds you hold, whenever a fleet is in orbit and not fighting. Independent class A yards build for anybody for 20% more. A new ship joins a Yard fleet in orbit when it is finished, and a notice at the start of your turn says so; how long it takes depends on the shipyard setting chosen at the start.

News and the fog of war

Unless you chose Instantly, news travels by ship. A report of anything happening away from your capital takes a week for every jump a courier would need to bring it: an enemy fleet arriving or leaving, a battle between other factions, a world changing hands. The map shows what your capital has heard. Other fleets appear where they were when last reported, and worlds show the owner the news last named.

Normally you see your own fleets and worlds as they are now, and news of anything they are part of reaches you at once: their battles, arrivals, sieges and captures. Your fleets do not wait for you either. They act on their standing orders the moment they meet the enemy.

Full fog of war

Turn on Full fog and your own fleets report by courier too. Each one appears where it was when its last report was sent, marked with how old that report is, and you hear of its battles when a courier gets home. Orders go the same way: an order to a fleet five weeks away lands five weeks later, and the fleet plans its route from wherever it has got to by then. Orders not yet delivered are listed on the fleet as in the post. A fleet lost in battle stays on your map until the news of it arrives. Splitting and merging fleets can only be done at your capital, where you can see the ships.

How fast news travels, and full fog, can both be changed at any time on the Empire tab, for every player at once. Turn full fog off and every order in the post arrives at once.

Winning

Hold the share of the peopled worlds in play chosen at the start, half unless you changed it, or be the last faction standing. A faction with no worlds and no ships is out. The Empire tab shows the standings and how many worlds you need.

Tips

  • Leave something at home. The computer will come for your capital, and it looks for weak spots.
  • Keep an eye on fuel. A fleet that runs dry at a world with no gas giant and a hostile starport is stuck.
  • Missile boats hit hard but run out. Send them home to reload, or mix them with lasers.
  • Big bays and spinal mounts are wasted on small ships; a swarm of fighters can tie them up.
  • Independent worlds near your Main are cheap growth early on. Enemy capitals are the hardest nuts in the sector.
  • A fleet that keeps losing ships is telling you something. Raise its withdrawal point.
  • The computer sees no more than you would in its place. It knows nothing of a system where it has no ships and no worlds.

Your own sectors and ships

Sectors. Make one in PlanetHex. When you save a sector there, it writes a .sec file beside the .sector file. In Mainline's new game screen, press Load a PlanetHex .sec… and pick it. Sparse or Rift density makes the best game. At Standard density the worlds join up so well that one Main can cover half the sector. Any other tool that writes the same tab-separated sector format works too.

Ships. Design one in the Traveller Ship Designer and save it as a .ship file. On the Shipyard tab, press Import a .ship design…. It joins the catalogue for this game and travels inside the saved game. A design has to be legal under the designer's rules. Everything Mainline knows about a ship comes off its design sheet: hull, armour, drives, fuel, weapons, software, sensors and price.
